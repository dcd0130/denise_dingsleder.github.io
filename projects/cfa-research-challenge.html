Overview

The CFA Institute Research Challenge is a global student competition in which teams research a real, publicly traded company and produce an institutional-quality equity research report with a Buy, Hold, or Sell recommendation, presented to a panel of investment professionals. My team of four was assigned R1 RCM, a healthcare revenue-cycle-management company, and placed 3rd in the State of Utah, representing Westminster College.

What We Built

My primary contributions within the team were the comparable-company (comps) valuation model, the financial ratio analysis, price-performance tracking against the S&P 500, and the risk-analysis framework that breaks the investment thesis into five categories: management, investment, industry, financial, and business risk.

What We Learned

  • Skills: comparable-company valuation, reading and modeling real financial statements, structuring a five-category risk framework, and presenting an investment thesis to a professional panel.
  • Where it's used: this is the same comps methodology sell-side equity research and buy-side analysts use every day to price a stock and defend a Buy/Hold/Sell call.
  • Reflection: it taught me how much judgment sits inside an "objective" valuation — which peers you pick and which multiple you weight changes the answer, so a defensible methodology matters as much as the final number.

Recommendation

Our team's final call was Sell, with a blended price target of $11.92 against a then-current price of $12.40 (about 4% downside). The target combined four valuation methods — a DCF with Monte Carlo sensitivity testing (40% weight), EV/EBITDA (30%), P/E (20%), and P/FCF (10%) — and the call was reinforced by the risk picture: debt nearly tripling to fund the Cloudmed acquisition just as interest rates rose, heavy revenue concentration in two customers (Ascension and Intermountain) with Ascension itself showing financial strain, a history of missing earnings in 5 of the last 7 periods, notable insider selling, and a high short interest of 14.45%.

This was a 4-person team effort as part of a class competition — the report, recommendation, and presentation were a group product, and the sections above reflect where I focused.

Technical Deep-Dive: Valuation Methodology

This report didn't run on a codebase — it's a research deliverable built in Excel and Word — but the valuation approach follows a standard institutional comps process:

  • Peer selection. Built a comparable-company set of healthcare revenue-cycle-management and services firms, screened for similar size, growth profile, and margin structure to R1 RCM.
  • Multiple analysis. Calculated trading multiples across the peer set (EV/EBITDA, P/E, EV/Revenue) and benchmarked R1 RCM against the peer median and quartile range.
  • Implied valuation. Applied the peer-median multiples to R1 RCM's own financials to back out an implied share-price range, then triangulated it against the ratio and price-performance analysis.
  • Risk-adjusted conviction. Weighed that implied range against the five-category risk framework (management, investment, industry, financial, business) to size the team's conviction behind the final Buy/Hold/Sell call.
